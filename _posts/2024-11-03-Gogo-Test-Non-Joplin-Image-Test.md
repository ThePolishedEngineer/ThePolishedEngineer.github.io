Meet Gogo.  For privacy reasons, I won’t be sharing a photo of her, but that photo up there is a fairly good approximation, even considering that it’s a silhouette rather than a photo. 

Gogo is a nearly 100-pound black lab mix.We adopted her when she was young - she was in training to be a service dog, but she was far too anxious and aggressive for public access work, and washed out. 

So we have a whip-smart, headstrong, anxious dog living with us. 

She loves her routines — she knows exactly when she’s supposed to get her meds, she refuses to go up to bed if we didnt take her to the back yard yet, and she can absolutely tell when we’ve short-changed her on treats for her standard tasks. 

She has some leftover tasks she learned from her service dog days, but because of her anxiety and behavioral issues, we’ve been having to redo more basic training with her too.

Lately, I’ve been working with her on communication training, offering her Food or Out or Treats, and having her nose my hand if that’s what she wants. I’d love to try communication buttons with her, but that’s a commitment we haven’t taken on yet. 

Most of my ramblings about Gogo’s training and behavior will probably be found in the Everyday Insights section. 

Gogo is a wonderful girl. An absolute menace, but a wonderful girl regardless. I am proud to say that I am the only person that Gogo will cuddle with. I love her very, very much.
